August

Ideation and identity

5 August

Where I sit in design

Much of the early thinking revolved around my place in the world of design. What job am I aiming for, and what kind of energy do I want to put out into the world? Starting a Figma and a PureRef board helped get the ideas moving.

Running alongside the capstone, as a transition to industry, I have also been working on my own personal branding. As with all creative work, the two bleed into each other.

Paradise, P. [@phoebe.paradise]. (2026, May 19). Finally got around to experimenting with the amazing Procreate Dreams while recovering from surgery and the more I worked [Video]. Instagram. instagram.com/reel/DYhFF6eTtkb

A few early ideas came out of Hello Stranger. That supermarket project has an interesting interactive stack: TouchDesigner and MediaPipe piped into Unreal over an OSC server, so the geometry, particles, lighting, materials and Blueprint logic can all be driven by outputs derived from a plain video feed. It is a fairly unusual way to build an environment.

The Paradise piece above was a good early reference for the potential of a plant driven by the detected user, with other data, such as the observers, driving the light behind it.

11 August

Prototypes

Getting the project running end to end is the biggest priority, so I can test both the functionality and the concept with people.

That meant a simple four-bone chain skinned to an object, mapped to mouse movement for now.

Had the usual trouble with scale and export settings, so smoothing those workflows out early is always worth it. Creating the Blueprint and the skeletal mesh is straightforward enough. Transforming and rotating bones can be driven by different values down the chain, and here I am multiplying the bend factor by increments.

On theme, the early visual tests have been looking at vivid values against shadow, used to good effect in Disney's Fantasia.

19 August

Finding the thread

Thinking about a more abstract way to present the scene and the object. A scribbly doodled style feels appropriate and a bit nostalgic. That kind of rendering could be faked with spline geometry and ribbon particles, though a custom HLSL shader is also an option for cross-hatching.

The second image is Spirals on Spirals, which throws out an interesting outward pattern. The high fluorescent blues, whites and greens could work as a dopamine pulse of happiness, going grey when still. The stippling reminds me of the cover of Shaun Tan's The Night, and it raises the idea of rendering the scene in a similar way: a hatch shader, or simple white on edge detection, possibly with a flipped and offset geometry trick.

23 August

Fixed digital twin

Taking inspiration from Universal Everything's Digital Twin campaign. I liked the idea of raycasting, which is expensive, or a particle explosion on an observed skeleton. A lot of what makes that campaign work is the simulated weight and bounce.

Sketching plants and weeds from my backyard as eclectic foliage figures. They could expand, grow and contract depending on how much movement or energy a user gives them. A generated surface populated with smaller plants, sudden blooms, tight spatial arrangements like phyllotaxis. If movement is slow, it leans toward broms, staghorn, old man's whiskers, trees.

Could be asset intensive.

Previz of a plant head in Blender. It should be possible to get a head bone and orientation from the distance between the ears, with nose tilt controlling different spheres of scattered sprite geometry.

Testing geometry nodes along a spline. Exploring coiling and uncoiling, and collapsing the geometry node setup into two states with morph targets blending between them.

29 August

Defining the interaction

After trying to talk the interaction through with peers in a tutorial, I found I could not explain it easily. That is a good sign that I need to think the whole thing through end to end rather than fixating on the visual language.

This was always partly a test of whether I could drive morph targets and bones from Unreal. The early version ran on deprojected mouse movement.

The current idea is a silhouette of my backyard represented in gradients, with an amorphous weed that moves and reacts. Still, it fades to grey. With movement, the visuals build.

Audience. General gallery visitors at the student showcase: friends, family, other students, some industry. People can engage as much or as little as they like, and watching is a legitimate way to experience it.

The piece is designed around the honeypot effect. People are drawn to interactive work by watching others use it, and the hard part is crossing from spectator to participant. The observer's behaviour is the interaction, whether or not they step forward.

Large movements unfurl the ribbons into something brief and joyous. Stillness is not a failure state. There is more to find for anyone who wants it, but the piece does not require it.

September

Form and procedural build

5 September

Nodes

Much of the work here has been rethinking the geometry node setup.

Getting the new geometry-node mesh to look at the cursor, which stands in for the user's head. Using absolute world space on Z is a highly effective way to get a hazy void. Artistically I am moving toward lit shaders, since pure emissive is a bit too formless at this stage. Materials need iterating sooner rather than later.

12 September

Expected response in the gallery

If someone chooses to stay with the piece, that should still be rewarded and not read as a negative encounter. After all, they are choosing to stay.

I will have to think about not making the neutral state too mundane, or reading as depressing.

14 September

Feedback

Refining the concept with Kali Maunder helped a great deal, thinking through the character from a traditional animator's perspective: the squash and tension in the body under anxiety, muscles bundling, hunched shoulders, eye stress lines.

That informed a fascicles setup in geometry nodes. A mesh curve becomes the stem's body, likely invisible, and the fascicles generate and twist around it. There is potential to have that effect drive morph targets on the character. World position offset might also work, though I know less about it at the point of converting to Unreal.

The bundle and twist is literal with the knots here. The radius stays the same at the base, with room to expand it later.

16 September

Planning eye states

The eye is becoming a bigger feature. Two-way blends on a Look At node made some interesting shapes, but a clean profile curve is more striking. Right now it snakes around every axis, which distorts the top-heavy shoulder curl that gives the thing character without being a direct human representation.

28 September

Character profiles

Adding a taper driven by a profile curve. It reads as a more interesting shape, and as more open and receptive. A stretch goal is to use Unreal's Niagara system to span extra meshes across the body. The shapes are good: when open, the tendrils almost look like a hand opening, or exposing an inside.

Most of my past animation experience was in Maya, and mostly simple work. Exploring Blender has been genuinely interesting in this regard, using actions, the dope sheet and the animation graphs with more purpose.

Rendering out a preview animation to share for feedback. With the weeks flying by, the scope is settling on two things:

  • Furl and unfurl based on presence, multiplied by motion.
  • Visuals still undecided. I like the flat look but have not explored custom GLSL or HLSL shaders. For now I like the flat saturated colour and the shared shader logic of world Z or height, going to a flat dark purple with possible hints of luminescent grass tips. I have a past animation of flat colours against a silhouette that could be striking and simple.

October

Interaction, build and exhibition

5 October

Geo Nodes, Unreal, TouchDesigner

This will be a good point to test with the cohort and with friends and family. The next phase is refining the data coming out of TouchDesigner.

I had been testing with a pre-recorded video stream decoded by MediaPipe, to confirm the movement actually reaches Unreal. The data dictates how reliably the interaction can be tested, so it needs more attention than I first gave it.

MediaPipe takes a webcam as its feed, and a live camera with real movement is better than a recording. OBS Studio can make a virtual camera from a looped file for the cases where I need something repeatable.

In the MediaPipe container, selecting and averaging the ears across X and Y on all four channels works better than the alternatives. MediaPipe also outputs a visibility channel for the ears, which makes presence easier to detect than I expected.

Currently a back and forth over how far a bone can coil in Blender. I used Claude to write a script that builds the skeleton uniformly along a spline. I had been building the chain as a straight vertical and then posing it, but that is not needed here.

AI use · bone chain generator

Tool

Claude (Anthropic), model claude-opus-5. Used to write the script below, which I then ran, read and adjusted in Blender.

Prompt

In Blender, I have a curve called Spline_Fiddle that describes the shape of a plant stem. Write a Python script that builds an armature along it with 24 bones, spaced evenly by arc length rather than by control point, each connected to the one before it and named stem_00 upward. I want the chain generated already in the curve's final shape rather than built vertical and posed afterwards.

Script

import bpy

CURVE  = "Spline_Fiddle"      # the curve the stem follows
ARM    = "ARM_Fiddle"
COUNT  = 24                   # bones in the chain
PREFIX = "stem_"

def sample_curve(obj, n):
    """n+1 points spaced evenly by arc length along the evaluated curve."""
    dg   = bpy.context.evaluated_depsgraph_get()
    ev   = obj.evaluated_get(dg)
    mesh = ev.to_mesh()
    pts  = [obj.matrix_world @ v.co for v in mesh.vertices]
    ev.to_mesh_clear()

    run = [0.0]                           # cumulative length at each vertex
    for a, b in zip(pts, pts[1:]):
        run.append(run[-1] + (b - a).length)
    total = run[-1]

    out, j = [], 0
    for i in range(n + 1):
        target = total * i / n
        while j < len(run) - 2 and run[j + 1] < target:
            j += 1
        span = run[j + 1] - run[j]
        f = 0.0 if span == 0 else (target - run[j]) / span
        out.append(pts[j].lerp(pts[j + 1], f))
    return out

curve = bpy.data.objects[CURVE]
pts   = sample_curve(curve, COUNT)

arm_data = bpy.data.armatures.new(ARM)
arm_obj  = bpy.data.objects.new(ARM, arm_data)
bpy.context.collection.objects.link(arm_obj)
bpy.context.view_layer.objects.active = arm_obj
bpy.ops.object.mode_set(mode='EDIT')

parent = None
for i in range(COUNT):
    b = arm_data.edit_bones.new(f"{PREFIX}{i:02d}")
    b.head = pts[i]
    b.tail = pts[i + 1]
    if parent:
        b.parent = parent
        b.use_connect = True
    parent = b

bpy.ops.object.mode_set(mode='OBJECT')
print(f"{COUNT} bones along {CURVE}")

What I changed

The first version spaced the bones by control point, which bunched them where the curve was dense and left the tip under-articulated. Resampling by arc length fixed it. I also had to set the armature modifier to the top of the modifier stack by hand, above GeometryNodes, or the deformation evaluated in the wrong order.

The curve has an interesting character to it. A bit like a warbled question mark.

A world position offset shader has been harder to get right than I thought. Presence and detection feel good, and I am leaning away from making a direct character. It is an assumption, but a character or a puppet tells you what it is, and that leaves less room for curiosity.

14 October

Touch measurements

Exploring the idea of the shared energy needed to make an interactive exhibit whole. Someone interacting with the exhibit is more or less what the exhibition is. The pressure to perform is shared by the weed, which oscillates between a subdued, guarded state and an open one depending on what it is given.

Changing from head to shoulders for the uncoiling. Much easier to measure the distance between those two points than the head, which can turn.

Select (left_shoulder:x, right_shoulder:x)
  → Math CHOP: Subtract, then Absolute Value
  → Filter 0.1s
  → Rename "proximity"
  → into the existing Merge

After discussing the project and thinking harder about expected behaviour from a participant, the coil and uncoil will not be driven by movement alone. Presence can still uncoil the weed.

Using Switch on String, I can filter out /head_tx, /head_ty and the rest, then set a variable of the same name. Presence arrives as something like 0.5336666, so bands of values between 0 and 1 can drive different parts of the animation and the scene.

Proximity needs proper calibration, ideally with the ability to change the numbers on the fly. That is the part that can only be done once, properly, in the actual room: stand where the furthest viewer will realistically be and note the value, then stand at the closest point that should count as fully engaged and note that.

Dialling in the interaction by clamping the spine so it moves predictably while keeping a distinct silhouette.

It is nice and simple as it stands, and I would like to explore other aspects of the interaction, particularly dynamic sound. The initial idea is sonification built from recordings of my backyard, with more ambient creature noises for the relaxed state, and loose against tight wire strums for the rest.

Shoulder width controls the coiling, open or engaged based on an estimate of body position. Pluck uses vertex world position offset linked to the wrists. I was wary of adding another form of interaction for a number of reasons, but it turned out to be the natural progression and was surprisingly fast to retool, since most of the logic already existed.

Having watched people use it, I am reluctant to add more variables, but the neon green pluck band needs separating from delta time so it responds to raw movement instead. Right now it fires more or less constantly, which drains it of meaning.

Added head tilt after thinking about the scene as a whole, so the creature reads as attending to someone rather than merely facing them.

18 October

Mote field

The mote field was far harder to set up and debug than I anticipated, given I could already track hands reliably with the debug sphere. It meant tightening the particle spawn bounds and revisiting the deprojected plane, visible above as the debug sphere.

The Blueprint looks complicated, but the system is really just filtering these values. Wrist movement still needs separating for the mote field, though with time running out I moved that to a nice-to-have.

22 October

The whole chain

Drawn out, the system is much smaller than the Blueprint suggests. Three numbers cross from TouchDesigner into Unreal and everything else is derived from them. Shoulder width replaced head tracking because the head turns and the shoulders do not. Movement is read as speed rather than position, so the plant answers to how you move instead of where you are.

24 October · after

What happened in the room

It worked. Friends, family and cohort rather than industry, but it worked. Fiddlearm opened for people who found the sweet spot, and others noticed from across the room that it was behaving differently for them than it had a moment earlier. That recognition, one person seeing another person's version of it, was the honeypot effect doing exactly what I had designed for.

I never got to fine-tune the calibration in the actual room. Flooding took that window away, and it showed. The band between nothing and fully open was narrower than it should have been, so the piece spent most of the night in the middle of its range rather than using the ends.

One person moved in large gestural ways. Only one. It was rare enough that I immediately wanted to give them a bigger payoff than I had built, and that gap is the clearest single lesson from the night. The rare engagement is the thing worth designing for, and I had under-rewarded it.

What I would change

Sound. It stayed on the list all semester and never got built. Even subtle audio would have cut through a noisy gallery, and it was the cheapest payoff available to me.

A bigger ceiling. The screen-buffer pulse, or a large background colour shift, would have given that one gestural visitor something that matched what they put in.

Head volume as the repeller. Very few people raised their hands, so the mote field rarely fired. Driving it from the head instead would have meant everyone saw it, and it would have made the head tracking legible at the same time, which was its own unsolved problem.

A light on the motes. An alpha light travelling with the particles would have made the interaction visible rather than merely present.

What I would keep

Not using a biped. A dancing figure in a T-pose would have been simpler to build and would have read instantly, and that is exactly the problem. A recognisably human form tells you what you are supposed to do with it. An amorphous one does not, and the not-knowing is the work. People experimented because there was nothing obvious to copy.

The jank, too. The calibration is imperfect, it closes when you do not expect it, and it does not always answer the way you think it should. That resistance is why people stayed with it rather than solving it in four seconds and walking off. There is no correct input, and the piece is better for refusing to pretend otherwise.

Where it goes

The obvious next version is scale. Four walls rather than one monitor, the way Hello Stranger ran, where the thing is around you rather than in front of you. The interaction logic would survive the move largely unchanged, since it only passes three numbers.

The less obvious one already exists. Fiddlearm is rebuilt on my own site as a rendered loop with the eye driven live, which is a cheap way to carry the idea into a browser where the real sensing cannot go. It is not the piece, but it holds the part that matters, which is something that notices you are there.

What the project turned out to be about is not the plant. It is the effort of staying open and curious with something that does not immediately reward you, rather than closing up and moving on. Watching people find that, and watching others not find it, was the most useful thing I did all semester.
