Process journal

Weird Weeds, from a moodboard in August to a presence-responsive creature on a gallery monitor in October. Kept as I went, trimmed afterwards.

August

Ideation and identity

5 August

Where I sit in design

Spent the first week on what story I want to tell and what interaction I want someone to have in front of the capstone. Looking to continue the thinking from Hello Stranger and its themes of non-places, inspired by how Airship Orchestra set up in those spaces and turn them into places of wonder. A piece that does that on a much smaller scale.

Started a Figma board for reference images and workshop notes, and did the first searches on naming and online footprint. My old branding was MJBissell, which feels like a real estate agent. Matty Bissell reads better, though MattyB is a large American YouTuber, so not a legal problem but a search one.

August

Wordmark

Doodling letterforms, looking at negative space for any interesting interplay. A lot of the hand-sketched versions spoke to the scramble-brain aesthetic that feels on brand, but in vector they looked plain.

The wordmark that worked is geometric outside with softer characterised inner shapes: nudges toward feet, limbs and a wink in the negative space and on the letter spines. BISSELL is Iosevka Charon Mono, an open-source monospace built for terminals, a solid anchor under the playful growth of the MATTY characters. A reverse mullet. Business underneath, party on top.

11 August

Touchpoints

Ethically and optically it is uncomfortable to be on LinkedIn given the current climate of big tech, but most of my past work and contacts are there, so it still needs updating. Stepping out of my comfort zone and using a portrait, very Rembrandt, who was a favourite growing up. Master of light and shadow, which became a large vector of development in 3D graphics as we moved to simulating photons and bounce light.

August

The statement

I came to design late, after a lot of other jobs. That history is the useful part. For a long time I thought being good at art meant being accurate, so I studied anatomy and Leonardo and drew realistically. My hand never agreed. The discipline stuck and still runs underneath everything, but it stopped being the output and became the foundation.

What I want to make now are non-places that stop being non-places. Airports, waiting rooms, service centres, foyers. Spaces built for utility, where people are processed rather than met. I want to put something in them that surprises someone into a bit of joy.

This is for organisations responsible for spaces people have to pass through, physical or digital. A council foyer and a government form at eleven at night are the same problem in different media.

22 August

Blender to Unreal

First skinned mesh export, working out naming conventions and structure. The settings that actually matter, since the defaults disagree between the two applications:

Apply Scalings   FBX All   Blender is metres, Unreal centimetres
Forward          X         Blender defaults to -Y, Unreal expects X
Primary Bone     X         matters when rotation is driven externally
Add Leaf Bones   Off       otherwise every chain gets a junk _end bone
Apply Transform  Off       inconsistent, and unnecessary after Ctrl+A

Apply all transforms before export. An unapplied non-uniform scale corrupts weight deformation and follows the asset into Unreal where it is much harder to diagnose. Three things confirm a good import: the mesh stands upright, scale is sensible against a default cube, and the Skeleton tab has no _end entries.

One petal to start, with a blueprint tracking the mouse cursor so the petals open on proximity. Get Mouse Position into Deproject Screen to World, then heavy interpolation because the raw mouse is far too fast.

Working on this I looked out the campus window at palm fronds dancing in the wind, which got me onto the tactile nature of vision, and what wind actually is as a thing you see rather than feel. The mouse-and-plant test gave me a stem with a look-at node orienting toward an interpolated cursor position, which TouchDesigner and MediaPipe will eventually drive with a head approximation.

August

Looking at shaders

Shadertoy is a good place to see what is possible in fragment shader code. One render had a greasy paint quality on a ball that is hard to replicate in computer graphics and close to how I picture the bloom on top of the stalk. Another, spirals on spirals, throws out high fluorescent blues and greens that could work as a dopamine pulse, going grey when still.

The stippling reminded me of the cover of Shaun Tan's The Night. A stag running at night, good anticipation, freedom and excitement to come, against an otherwise inky environment. That inkiness is a direction worth holding onto.

August

Weird weed orchestra

Playing with a digital twin of my own yard. Lots of small repetitious granular flowers, large blooms unfurling like peacock feathers with high upbeat notes, lower trombones for the succulents and broms that spawn and shrink back down. More movement pushes the orchestra further. Distilling the happiness of the yard down into textures and materials, aiming at energy and growth, delicate under movement and hardy without it.

August

Gallery constraints

Practical considerations for the UniSC Art Gallery: power, a plinth to hide the laptop, webcam placement, networking, and the acoustics if there is audio.

Audience is general gallery visitors at the student showcase. Friends, family, other students, some industry. People can engage as much or as little as they like, and watching is a legitimate way to experience it. The piece is designed around the honeypot effect: people are drawn to interactive work by watching others use it, and the hard part is crossing from spectator to participant. The observer's behaviour is the interaction whether or not they step forward. Stillness is not a failure state.

September

Form and procedural build

September

Depth from a line drawing

Fed a loose line drawing through Depth Anything V2. Fascinating to watch it interpret something that imprecise. Opens a possible direction around gesture-based drawing processed into a 3D interpretation.

13 September

What the weeds look like

Still quite fluid on the specific visuals, but gravitating toward procedural generation on the stems in Blender, which is both practical and a bit of fun. Heavily inspired by Universal Everything's technical explosion in their characters.

14 September

An animator's read on anxiety

Refining the concept with Kali Maunder helped a great deal, thinking through the character from a traditional animator's perspective: the squash and tension in the body under anxiety, muscles bundling, hunched shoulders, eye stress lines.

That informed a fascicles setup in Geometry Nodes. A mesh curve becomes the stem's body, likely invisible, and the fascicles generate and twist around it. The bundle and twist is literal with the knots, radius staying the same at the base with potential to expand.

28 September

The eye becomes the feature

Got the new geometry-node mesh looking at the cursor, standing in for the viewer's head. Using absolute world space on Z is a highly effective hazy void, and I am moving toward lit shaders since pure emissive is a bit too formless at this stage.

Two-way blends on a look-at node made some interesting shapes, but a clean profile curve is more striking. Right now it snakes around every axis, which distorts the top-heavy shoulder curl that gives the thing character without being a direct human representation.

Added a taper driven by a profile curve, which reads as a more interesting shape and also as more open and receptive. When open, the tendrils almost look like a hand opening, or exposing an inside.

So far I have been happy with how quick the testing and prototyping has been. In the past I would have jumped straight to rigging a character flower. Quick iterative procedural tests are a better way to find the balance of control between the two.

September

Interaction states, first pass

Rendering a preview animation for feedback. The interaction as I have it so far:

  • Idle — coiled, tense and wary. Observers detected.
  • Curious — unfurls to 50% when a skeleton is detected. Tracks orientation with look-at nodes. Bone rotation dampens when curled, but the eye is independent and always tracks.
  • Movement — flashes like a snapshot, saturation driven by velocity.
  • Dance — unfurls from 50 to 100% under a lot of motion.

It always seeks to return to a 25 or 50% baseline. I am concerned this is already too many layers and risks overcomplicating what should be tense and alone moving to relaxed and free.

October

Interaction, build and exhibition

1 October

Rebinding the eye

Rebound the eye socket to the correct mesh. Vertex weight paint is still offset. Onto the next phase: getting the interaction working out of TouchDesigner.

6 October

TouchDesigner to Unreal

Three outputs from TouchDesigner on port 10000, sending normalised screen coordinates, since I already have the systems built around the deprojected plane intersection.

/head_tx   0..1   head centre, horizontal
/head_ty   0..1   head centre, vertical
/presence  0|1    is anyone there

MediaPipe takes a webcam feed, and a live camera is better than prerecorded video for testing. OBS Studio makes a virtual camera from a looped file for when I need something repeatable. Selecting and averaging the ears across X and Y on all four channels works better than the alternatives here, and MediaPipe's visibility channel on the ears makes presence easier to detect than I expected.

13 October

Back into it

Coming back to the project after a while is discombobulating. Good to do some motion work on Metamorphosis to get moving again.

14 October

Didactics, and moving to the shoulders

Working on the didactic. Exploring the idea of the shared energy required to make an interactive exhibit whole: someone interacting with the exhibit is more or less what the exhibition is. The pressure to perform is shared by the weed.

Changed the uncoiling driver from head to shoulders. Much easier to measure the distance between those two points than the head, which can turn.

Select (left_shoulder:x, right_shoulder:x)
  → Math CHOP: Subtract, then Absolute Value
  → Filter 0.1s
  → Rename "proximity"
  → into the existing Merge

After talking the project through and thinking harder about expected behaviour from a participant, coil and uncoil will not be driven by movement alone. Presence can still uncoil the weed.

Proximity needs proper calibration, ideally with numbers I can change on the fly. The part that can only be done once, properly, in the actual room: stand where the furthest viewer will realistically be and note the value, then stand at the closest point that should count as fully engaged and note that.

October

Pluck

Wrist positions now drive a pluck on the arm. Routing TouchDesigner to OSC into Unreal through a switch on the string is a much more elegant way to test several interaction types in quick order.

Three channels now sit together cleanly. Shoulder width controls coiling. The spine is clamped so it moves predictably while keeping a distinct silhouette. Pluck uses vertex world position offset linked to the wrists. I was wary of adding another form of interaction at this point, but it turned out to be the natural progression and surprisingly fast to retool given most of the logic already existed.

Calling the character Fiddlearm, which has a nice ring to it.

Rather than complicating the interaction further there is a nice simplicity to it. It would benefit from art passes and shader work, but I would rather spend the remaining time on other aspects of interaction, particularly sound.

October

Sound, in theory

I keep coming back to this. At one point Fiddlearm was almost an instrument you could play through the strums on tense and loose strings. Now I think it is closer to an alert ambience, the kind that exists on dusk or when the night is young and everything is in transit.

With no user detected, or someone turned away, the sound is the gallery ambience itself. On showcase night that will be loud, but more often it is HVAC and projectors. Low reverb strums as it opens, twangier noises when tense, city honks and aeroplanes under apprehension, tranquil night sounds when open. The piece exists visually with nobody there, but it does not really appear until someone is present, and the audio should follow the same rule, affecting not just the weed but the world it sits in.

October

Testing on family

Most of the testing has been with cohort and family. People are far more likely to interact with it than I thought, though they often try sudden movements rather than exploring body position, and they do tend to turn to face the camera.

My wife wanted it to track her movements and almost became self-conscious when it did not. An impromptu test with my daughter, who still does not like the interaction, did reveal that shoulder width for small children is a problem.

The green pluck is currently firing constantly. It needs separating from delta time and responding to raw movement instead.

October

Motes, and getting hands into the scene

A component missing from the scene is a way for the viewer's raw interaction to appear without overpowering what is already there. Tracking hand positions and having them push soft motes around could orient the user and give a sense of being a force within the scene rather than only being seen by it.

Risky to add this many new elements at this stage, so I took a restore point with working TouchDesigner and Unreal packages first.

Hands were harder than expected. The mote field attracts or repels from the deprojected position, but driving it from visibility alone caused odd gating. Moved the particles back to 100 and matched the deprojection to the same location. Wrist movement currently fires strength on both wrists, which would mean separating them in TouchDesigner, but the particles are reacting.

It added real time at a critical point, but having arms in the scene is a much more natural way to get granular feedback without overpowering anything. Eventually both wrists should be driven by their own movement and velocity, and the wrists should carry an alpha, possibly the eye itself acting as a fake light.

24 October

Exhibition

It worked out well. Friends and family, not really any industry people, but it was good. Watching people with the interaction was interesting. I failed to fine-tune the calibration in the room, flooding prevented that honestly, but it still worked in the sense that Fiddlearm opened for people who found the sweet spot, and others noticed from across the room that it was behaving differently for them.

I only really saw one person moving and interacting in large gestural ways, which was rare enough that I wanted to give them a bigger payoff. Audio would have helped, and even subtle audio would cut through a noisy gallery. That was the moment I regretted not having the screen-buffer pulse or large background colour changes, which would have been very eye-catching there.

Very few people raised their hands at all, which was fine, but it would have benefited from the head volume acting as the particle repeller. That might also have solved the head tracking, with people realising it tracks the head. An alpha light on the particles so it appeared to be highlighting them, plus a synapse burn on the background frame buffer, would have made a much stronger piece for anyone who did perform strongly.

The rarity of going beyond 0 to 1 can definitely be strengthened, but it was still fun, and it brought a lot of joy to watch people engage. Recognising the disappointment when they did not is part of what makes the rarer engagements feel unique.

Another point of pride, or at least sticking to my guns, is that the observed testing did not rely on a biped character. If you were a weed dancing in a T-pose, that would have been far simpler for a technical showcase. Would it have sparked more joy? I resisted it because a more amorphous creature does not immediately tell you what you should do. Copy human-centric features and it becomes a less intentional experience.

It would be interesting to see this on a much grander version. Four walls.

October

Fiddlearm on the web

An interesting blend: a rendered loop with the live interaction limited to the eye. A low-cost way to recreate Fiddlearm in a form that works in a browser, which is now the hero of this site.
